As a control, Prophet fitted once and forecasting the full 52 weeks scored 268.2% WAPE. The 167-point gap against the rolling-origin version quantifies how much of Prophet's disadvantage was the harder task rather than the method.

**Improvement over 4-week moving average:** +6.3 WAPE points (+8.2% relative).

**Validation vs. test:** Validation WAPE (62.8%, on 8 held-out weeks inside training) is 7.5 points better than test WAPE (70.3%, the 2010-2011 period). The model generalises well within the training distribution; the gap reflects year-over-year distribution drift, with total demand down 26.4% in the test period. This is the case for scheduled retraining rather than for additional regularisation.

## Findings

Prophet and seasonal naive are the only two methods above 100% WAPE, and both carry large positive bias (+142, +118). Both estimate an annual seasonal component from a single year of per-product history and over-forecast into a year of declining demand. LightGBM pools all 50 series into one model, learning shared seasonal structure from 2,600 observations rather than 52 per product -- that pooling is the main reason it wins.

## Limitations

- MAPE is unreliable on this dataset: a diagnostic pass (see `scripts/diagnose_mape.py`) found it dominated by a handful of low-volume, erratic rows rather than reflecting typical forecast quality. WAPE is the metric to trust; MAPE is reported for reference only.
- Results come from a single train/test split (52 weeks each), not repeated cross-validation across multiple periods, so these numbers carry the uncertainty of one historical split rather than an averaged estimate.
- Total demand fell 26.4% from train to test (see 'Validation vs. test' above): this is distribution drift, not overfitting, and argues for scheduled retraining as the test period ages further.
- The top-50 product set excludes single-line-dominated and thin-training-history products (see `demand.py`); results do not necessarily generalize to the full product catalog.
- Prophet is fit per-product on at most 52 weeks of history -- short for learning a yearly seasonal component, which likely explains its weaker performance here relative to the lag/rolling-feature approach.
